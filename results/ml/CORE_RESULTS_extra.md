## Reading the results honestly

**Does the CNN beat the baselines?** Not on any of the three pre-registered metrics against the best honest baseline, and it does not lose either: every paired interval against the best baseline includes zero.

* **P1 (primary).** CNN 0.739 (seeds 0.752, 0.717, 0.720) against the v1 logistic chain 0.796 and the v2 chain 0.772, XGBoost 0.736 to 0.763. The CNN's interval overlaps all of them. It beats the ratio-of-ratios rules and the clock-only baseline, which are not strong baselines. With 6 people the interval is wide by construction (declared in the pre-registration). Per person the CNN ranks hypoxic windows well (plain AUROC 0.91 to 0.99 for each held-out person, seed 0), but its fixed 0.5 threshold is miscalibrated for some people (specificity 0.34 for one person), which is what the time-matched metric punishes.
* **Hybrid and adaptation (exploratory, table above).** Stacking the CNN logit with the v1 chain logit through a meta-learner fit on inner out-of-fold predictions gives 0.796, the same as the chain alone (AUROC 0.893 against 0.887). Choosing the candidate by inner CV gives 0.802 and mostly selects the chain itself, so on these 6 people the CNN adds no measurable information beyond the engineered chain. Test-time BatchNorm adaptation on the person's own first minute lowered the CNN (0.752 to 0.682 with a 50 % blend and 0.638 with a full blend), so it was not adopted. Averaging the logits of the 3 seeds gives 0.754 (CI 0.573 to 0.820), no gain beyond seed noise.
* **P2.** On 100 people at full coverage the CNN is within 5 bpm on 0.414 of the windows (LOSO), the v2 spectral estimator on 0.419 (difference -0.005, interval -0.055 to 0.043). By dataset the CNN is worse on MTHS (0.523 against 0.633) and better on BUT (0.355 against 0.304); the per-dataset paired intervals were not tested and the BUT gain should not be claimed.
* **P3.** The CNN's MTHS SpO2 MAE is 1.426 against 1.354 for the train-mean predictor (difference -0.072, interval -0.280 to 0.118): no skill over a constant on a normoxic cohort, as the pre-registration anticipated. It is clearly better than the textbook ratio-of-ratios rules (3.3 and 6.0). MTHS is not evidence that the SpO2 head works; the absolute-SpO2 test that matters is the ABG-anchored OpenOximetry evaluation (not part of this feature).
* **What the ablations say (descriptive; 6 people, several variants, selection optimism applies).** Removing OpenOx data (row 4) drops P1 from 0.739 to 0.577 and P2 from 0.469 to 0.349 (grouped protocol): the OpenOx supervised warm-up (HR, low-oxygen and SaO2 labels) is the main source of the CNN's skill, not the phone data alone. SSL pretraining alone (row 2: stage B without SSL) changes P1 by 0.013 (0.726 against 0.739, intervals overlap) and helps HR (P2 0.383 against 0.469). No pretraining at all (row 10) is far worse (P1 0.550, P2 0.220). Physics channels (row 3) change nothing measurable on P1 (0.731 against 0.739) and P2; baseline-relative conditioning (row 6) lowers P1 to 0.711 with a much larger train-test gap (0.223 against 0.097); device augmentation (row 5) is worth +0.019 on P1 and nothing on P2 (not significant, so the device-agnostic augmentation claim is **not** supported by this data beyond a small, uncertain gain). An HR-only CNN (row 7) is as good at HR as the multi-task one (0.501 against 0.469, P1 and P3 undefined), so multi-task training does not help HR here. A 20 s window (row 8, MTHS only for P2) gives 0.580 against 0.566 on the same MTHS windows. The gradient-reversal head (row 9, exploratory) gives 0.749 on P1 and 0.496 on P2, within noise of the full model (single seed).
* **Train-vs-test gap.** P1 0.097 (train time-matched balanced accuracy minus held-out, per-person), P2 0.003 to -0.007, P3 0.31 to 0.44 MAE points (test worse than train): the SpO2 head over-fits the training people.

## Limits that apply to every number above

* Only 6 people with real phone-camera SpO2 below 90 % exist; P1 intervals are wide and "tie" mostly means "cannot distinguish".
* One recipe (`C_lowhi`, chosen by nested CV on the Hoffman inner folds with a composite score) was applied to every fold and every variant; ablation variants were not re-tuned, which favours the full model. Rows with one seed (hr_only, win20, adv) have no seed SD.
* Ablation P2/P3 rows use 10 subject-grouped folds ("not LOSO") for compute; the `full` row appears under both protocols. The LOSO `full` row has seed 0 only for P2/P3 (seeds 1 and 2 were run for P1 and for the grouped protocol only).
* The first run of row 2 (`no_ssl`) was wrong: a variant-definition bug dropped the stage-B init, so it measured "no pretraining at all". It was found from the implausible gap, fixed (regression test `test_variants_keep_the_intended_init_and_switches`), rerun, and the buggy run is reported as row 10 (`no_pretrain`).
* BUT SpO2 is a spot value per person and is used only as a low-weight training label; no BUT SpO2 result is reported. The `dt` channel is zero in all real data (no phone dataset has frame timestamps); irregular timing is only simulated in augmentation.
* OpenOximetry is contact red/IR PPG: it supports pretraining and auxiliary heads but not phone SpO2 validation. 34 OpenOx people are held out of everything for later OpenOx-only tasks (`train.openox_holdout`); the "466 desaturations / 144 people" figure could not be traced, the data has 164 clock-aligned recordings (93 people).
* The input scale is per domain (phone, contact), not per dataset, so that it folds exactly into the exported weights; early stopping removes 1 Hoffman, 6 MTHS and 4 BUT people from each fold's training set.
* Licences: weights trained on MTHS (CC BY-NC-ND, non-commercial research) and on OpenOximetry (PhysioNet credentialed data) must be flagged in `DATA_LICENSES.md` when they ship. MTHS per-window predictions stay in the cache (`ml_cache/scores/cnn_*_mths.csv`); no OpenOx row is written anywhere.

## Prior work behind each ingredient (to be verified against the original papers before publication; none of these is claimed as new)

SimCLR contrastive learning (Chen et al., ICML 2020); gradient-reversal domain adaptation (Ganin and Lempitsky, ICML 2015); test-time BatchNorm statistics adaptation (Schneider et al., NeurIPS 2020; Nado et al., 2020); photoplethysmography foundation models and SSL on PPG (Abbaspourazad et al., ICLR 2024; Pillai et al., 2024); smartphone camera oximetry on the UW Hoffman data (Hoffman et al., npj Digital Medicine 2022). The combination used here (per-person first-minute baseline-relative inputs, a contact-PPG supervised warm-up feeding a phone-camera student, and the time-matched metric) is the project's own design and its measured benefit over the engineered chain is nil on P1, so no novelty claim is made for accuracy.
