## What was run

* **Pipeline** (`python/oxydrop_ml/`): `ssl_pretrain.py` (contrastive SSL) -> `train.py --stageb` (OpenOx supervised warm-up) -> `loso_run.py` (per-fold fine-tuning on pooled phone + OpenOx windows, LOSO or grouped folds,
  nested selection) -> `core_report.py` (tables, paired bootstrap, scores); `stack_run.py` (hybrid chain + CNN, BatchNorm adaptation). Student = `OxyNet` with 167,588 shipped parameters (`model.py`).
* **Per fold**: the held-out person (both Hoffman hands together) and an inner-validation set drawn from the other people (1 Hoffman, 6 MTHS, 4 BUT people; the same people in every fold, so the same Hoffman person
  is the early-stopping person in 5 of 6 P1 folds and each P1 model trains on 4 Hoffman people) never enter training, the input scale, the baseline-embedding table or the OpenOx warm-up. 400 fine-tuning steps
  (batch 128, AdamW lr 1e-3, cosine) from the stage-B checkpoint; the weights of the best inner-validation step are kept.
* **Pretraining never touches an outer test person**: the corpus holds no Hoffman, MTHS or BUT person (subject-prefix filter and dataset provenance, not a person-level cross-check); 34 OpenOx people (about 25 %, stratified by Monk tone,
  `train.openox_holdout`) are excluded from SSL, stage B and every supervised run so later OpenOx-only tasks can use people the student never saw. `tests/test_train.py` shows that changing a held-out person's signal and labels
  leaves every trained weight byte-identical, and that stage B and nested selection read only the rows they should.
* **Recipe selection (deviates from the pre-registered wording, see PREREGISTRATION.md Amendment 1)**: 4 candidate recipes were scored by inner folds (for each of the 6 outer Hoffman people: 2 inner Hoffman people, plus 8 MTHS and 6 BUT validation
  people; score = mean of time-matched balanced accuracy, HR within-5 and 1 - SpO2 MAE/5). The choice for one outer person uses only its own inner jobs (`nested_selection.csv`: 5 of 6 outer people pick `C_lowhi`, hof_100002 picks `D_steps800`). The single
  recipe applied to all folds and ablations (`C_lowhi`: low-oxygen loss weight 3) is the mean over ALL outer folds' inner scores, which scores every Hoffman person as an inner person, and the 14 MTHS/BUT validation people are P2/P3 evaluation people.
  `nested_selection_sensitivity.csv` shows that dropping every selection job that touches any one person never changes the winner. Any bias favours the CNN slightly and cannot create a win against a baseline by itself.
* **Protocols**: P1 is true LOSO over the 6 Hoffman people for every row. P2/P3: the `full` row is true LOSO over 100 people (62 MTHS + 38 BUT; P3 uses the 61 MTHS people that have a window at t >= 30 s); ablation rows use 10 subject-grouped folds over the same people
  and are labelled "not LOSO" in the protocol column (compute); `full` is also reported under that protocol. LOSO and grouped P2 differ by more than seed noise (see table) and are not interchangeable.
* **Seeds**: `full` has seeds 0, 1, 2 for P1 and for the grouped P2/P3 protocol; the number of seeds of the LOSO P2/P3 rows is in the seeds column (pre-registration rule 7 asks for 3: see the table and Amendment 1). Ablation rows have seed 0 for P2/P3
  and 3 seeds for P1 except `hr_only`, `win20`, `adv`. Single-seed rows should be compared with the `full (seed 0)` row.
* **Windows**: P1 = 5,644 Hoffman windows (t >= 60 s), decision = low-oxygen probability > 0.5 (fixed, not tuned), time-matched balanced accuracy pooled over people; P2 = every window with a reference HR of the 100 people
  at full coverage (no abstention); P3 = MTHS windows with t >= 30 s.
* **Train-vs-test gap**: each fold model is scored on its own training rows (P1: time-matched balanced accuracy on the training Hoffman people, pooled; P2: within-5 on up to 1,500 training MTHS+BUT windows) and the mean over folds is compared with
  the held-out score pooled over all held-out people. The training MAE of P3 in these runs lacked the t >= 30 s filter, so no P3 gap is reported (fixed in `train_scores` for future runs).

## Reading the results

Every number here appears in a table above (or in the CSV it is generated from).

**Does the CNN beat the baselines?** Not by the pre-registered rule on any of the three metrics. Against the best honest baseline of each task the paired interval of the 3-seed ensemble includes zero, so P1, P2 and P3 are ties; for P1 one seed (seed 1) loses to `chain_v1` and `chain_v2` on its own, so the CNN is not reliably as good as the engineered chain.

* **P1 (primary, 6 people).** Single-seed CNN values are 0.752, 0.717 and 0.748 (mean 0.739); the seed ensemble is 0.754. The v1 chain is 0.796, v2 chain 0.772, XGBoost 0.736 to 0.763. The CNN is better than the ratio-of-ratios rules and the clock-only baseline (weak baselines; not pre-registered wins). The per-person table shows why the seeds differ: the ranking of hypoxic windows is excellent for most people (AUROC 0.91 to 0.99) but seed 1 almost never raises an alert for hof_100001 (sensitivity 0.04, AUROC 0.69) and ranks hof_100004 near chance (AUROC 0.56). At the fixed 0.5 threshold specificity is as low as 0.34 for hof_100003; the time-matched metric punishes this.
* **Hybrid and adaptation (exploratory, seed 0).** A logistic meta-learner on [CNN logit, chain logit] fit on inner out-of-fold predictions gives 0.796, equal to the chain alone (plain AUROC 0.944 against 0.947 for the chain; TM AUROC 0.893 against 0.887). Selecting the candidate by inner CV gives 0.802 and picks the chain for 3 of 6 outer people, the BatchNorm-adapted stack for 2 and the plain CNN for 1. Test-time BatchNorm adaptation on its own lowers the CNN (0.752 to 0.682 with a 50 % blend and 0.638 with a full blend); it only helps inside the stack. On these 6 people the CNN adds no measurable information beyond the engineered chain; no interval was computed for the stack-minus-chain difference.
* **P2 (100 people).** Pooled within-5 bpm at full coverage: CNN 0.414, 0.435, 0.490 for seeds 0, 1, 2 (mean 0.447; the seed ensemble differs from the v2 spectral estimator, 0.419, by +0.047 [-0.007, 0.097]). Seed 2 alone is a win by the interval, seeds 0 and 1 are ties, so no win is claimed. By dataset (descriptive, not pre-registered): MTHS CNN 0.555 against 0.633 for v2 (a loss on seeds 0 and 1, tie on seed 2), BUT CNN 0.388 against 0.304 for v2 and 0.315 for the peak counter. Doubling is rare. LOSO (mean 0.447) and the grouped protocol (0.469) overlap within seed spread (0.414 to 0.490 and 0.445 to 0.491).
* **P3 (61 MTHS people with a window at t >= 30 s).** CNN MAE 1.457 (seeds 1.426, 1.438, 1.506) against 1.354 for the train-mean predictor and 1.351 for LightGBM: a tie in every seed (interval includes zero), i.e. no skill over a constant on a normoxic cohort, as the pre-registration expected. It is clearly better than the textbook ratio-of-ratios rules (3.3 and 6.0). MTHS is not evidence that the SpO2 head works; the absolute-SpO2 test that matters is the ABG-anchored OpenOximetry evaluation, which is not part of this feature.
* **What the ablations say (descriptive; 6 people, several variants; compare single-seed rows with `full (seed 0)`).** Removing OpenOx (row 4) drops P1 from 0.739 to 0.577 and P2 (grouped, seed 0) from 0.491 to 0.349; stage B without SSL (row 2) is 0.726 on P1 and 0.383 on P2, and no pretraining at all (row 10) is 0.550 and 0.220. So the OpenOx supervised warm-up is the main source of the CNN's skill, SSL on top of it gives a small P1 change inside the noise (0.739 against 0.726) and a clearer HR gain (0.491 against 0.383, single seed). Physics channels (row 3: 0.731 on P1) and device augmentation (row 5: 0.720) change P1 by less than the seed SD plus CI width, so the device-agnostic augmentation claim is **not** supported beyond a small, uncertain gain. Removing baseline-relative inputs (row 6) gives P1 0.711 with a large train-test gap (0.105 against -0.014). An HR-only CNN (row 7) matches the multi-task one on HR (0.501 against seed-0 0.491). The 20 s window (row 8, MTHS only for P2) gives 0.580 on MTHS against 0.588 for `full` seed 0 on the same windows (no gain). The gradient-reversal head (row 9, exploratory, one seed) is within noise.
* **Train-vs-test gap (matching aggregation).** P1 -0.015 (train time-matched balanced accuracy 0.724 against the pooled held-out 0.739, so no over-fitting signal on P1), P2 +0.055. P3 gap not reported (training MAE did not use the t >= 30 s filter).

## Limits that apply to every number above

* Only 6 people with real phone-camera SpO2 below 90 % exist; P1 intervals are wide and a "tie" mostly means "cannot distinguish". Seed-to-seed spread on P1 (SD 0.019) and on P2 (0.039) is of the order of several ablation effects.
* One recipe (`C_lowhi`, chosen by nested CV with the deviations of Amendment 1) was applied to every fold and variant; ablation variants were not re-tuned, which favours the full model. Rows with one seed have no seed SD.
* P2/P3 ablation rows use 10 subject-grouped folds ("not LOSO"). The `full` row has 3 seeds under both protocols.
* The first run of row 2 (`no_ssl`) was wrong: a variant-definition bug dropped the stage-B init, so it measured "no pretraining at all". It was found from the implausible gap, fixed (regression test `test_variants_keep_the_intended_init_and_switches`), rerun, and the buggy run is reported as row 10 (`no_pretrain`).
* BUT SpO2 is a spot value per person, used only as a low-weight training label; no BUT SpO2 result is reported. The `dt` channel is zero in all real data (no phone dataset has frame timestamps); irregular timing is only simulated in augmentation.
* OpenOximetry is contact red/IR PPG: it supports pretraining and auxiliary heads but not phone SpO2 validation. 34 OpenOx people are held out of everything for later OpenOx-only tasks (`train.openox_holdout`); the "466 desaturations / 144 people" figure could not be traced, the data has 164 clock-aligned recordings (93 people) with labels.
* `student_v1.pt` (the full recipe fit on all but the 11 inner-validation people) has seen 5 of the 6 Hoffman people: Hoffman metrics must not be quoted from it. Its weights are trained on MTHS (CC BY-NC-ND, non-commercial research) and OpenOximetry (PhysioNet credentialed data) and must be flagged in `DATA_LICENSES.md` when shipped. MTHS per-window predictions stay in the cache; no OpenOx row is written anywhere.
* **Superseded, state at the time of the run (written before FEAT-009).** Update 2026-11-15: FEAT-009 wired the model into the app. The app now runs the hybrid (v1 chain + residual CNN, `app/js/hybrid_engine.js`, trained `app/data/hybrid_R_s*.json` members) by default, and `app/data/cnn_weights.json` no longer exists. What this report said when it was generated: the CNN was not yet wired into the app (`app/js/cnn.js` existed and passed parity with `student_v1` at 8.0e-6, but nothing imported it and `app/data/cnn_weights.json` was still the random-init placeholder). That was FEAT-009.

## Prior work behind each ingredient (verify against the original papers before publication; none is claimed as new)

SimCLR contrastive learning (Chen et al., ICML 2020); gradient-reversal domain adaptation (Ganin and Lempitsky, ICML 2015); test-time BatchNorm statistics adaptation (Schneider et al., NeurIPS 2020; Nado et al., 2020); photoplethysmography foundation models and SSL on PPG (Abbaspourazad et al., ICLR 2024; Pillai et al., 2024); smartphone camera oximetry on the UW Hoffman data (Hoffman et al., npj Digital Medicine 2022). The combination used here (per-person first-minute baseline-relative inputs, a contact-PPG supervised warm-up feeding a phone-camera student, the time-matched metric) is the project's own design; its measured accuracy benefit over the engineered chain is nil on P1, so no novelty claim is made for accuracy.
