OxyDrop

Does your blood oxygen look lower than your own baseline? A phone-camera check, no extra hardware.

This project is a prototype and is not medical advice.

It is a research demo validated on only 6 people. It does not measure SpO₂, it is not a medical device, and it must never replace a pulse oximeter or a clinician. If you feel short of breath, confused, or unwell, get medical help now.

What it does

Rest for 60 seconds so it learns your normal. Then, every 2 seconds, it shows one of three results: Normal vs your baseline, Possible low oxygen: re-check with a pulse oximeter (a drop toward SpO₂ below 90%), or Unsure, re-measure. It shows “unsure” and no heart-rate number whenever the signal is poor, you move, the camera stutters, or the pulse cannot be locked.

  • Works only seated at rest. Exercise raises heart rate and causes false alarms.
  • Detects a drop from your own first minute. It cannot tell if you are already low at the start.
  • Validated on only 6 people. It cannot follow a resting heart rate below about 51 bpm (it says “unsure”).
  • Your camera video never leaves your phone. Everything runs in this page.

How it works

A small chain of stages turns camera frames into a decision: average colour per frame, a band-pass filter, beat detection (heart rate, variability, breathing, pulse strength), red/green light-absorption features, subtraction of your first-minute median, then two logistic regressions averaged. The whole model is 62 numbers. The data-rich stages carry the signal because only 6 people with phone video and real SpO₂ below 90% exist in open data.

Held-out accuracy on those 6 people (each tested with a model fitted on the other 5): 87.0% on the 82% of windows where the pulse locks; 94.7% on the 68% of windows where it is also confident enough to answer. One person (resting heart rate 43 bpm) never locks, so every window for them is “unsure”. A clock-only baseline gets 83.5%, but only 52% once time is controlled. Exercise falsely flags 63% of windows. See the project page for the full audit.

Set up your fingertip

  1. Sit down and rest for 2 minutes. Rest the phone and your hand on a table.
  2. Lay your fingertip flat across the whole camera plateau, covering the lens the app picks and, if you can, the flash under the same finger. Light pressure, do not press hard. Phones differ: on newer iPhone Pro models the flash is 2-3 cm from the main lens, so a flat finger over both gives a weaker but usable glow.
  3. Keep still. Don’t talk. Keep the screen on. After you tap the button the app tests every camera for about 3 seconds each and picks the one with the strongest pulse. If no back lens works it switches to the front camera with a white screen.
